1. The big picture

ERGO Connect is one web platform where ERGO, its customers (who need parts) and its suppliers (who make parts) work together, from the first request for a price to the delivered and closed order. Everyone signs in to the same system, but each person sees only what belongs to their own company and their own job.

ERGO is the middle man. The customer never deals with the supplier directly, and the supplier never learns who the customer is. Every hand-over passes through ERGO, which checks it, prices it and records it.

Customer RFQ → ERGO review → Parts sent to one or several suppliers → Supplier quotes → ERGO compares and selects one quote per part → ERGO's own quotation → Customer decision
↓
Sample PO → KAM creates the project → Plan to supplier → Supplier submits plan → ERGO agrees → Execution → Quality gate → Customer final approval
↓
Production PO → KAM creates the production project → Plan, execution, quality, approval → Order → Material, production, packing → Inspection passed → Dispatch, delivery, closure

Seven rules that hold everywhere

RuleWhat it means in practice
1. ERGO stays in the middleCustomers never see supplier names or supplier quotes. Suppliers never see the customer's name, the target price, other suppliers' quotes or ERGO's margin. Only ERGO sees both sides. This is enforced by the system itself, not just by hiding buttons.
2. The customer owns the RFQERGO cannot change a customer's RFQ. If something must change, ERGO asks the customer, who edits it and sends it again. ERGO keeps its own internal notes, priority and payment terms next to the RFQ.
3. One quote per part reaches the customerA part can go to several suppliers at once. ERGO compares their quotes and selects one for each part. Only ERGO's own quotation for that selected quote is sent to the customer, never the supplier's document and never the other quotes.
4. Two checks on every actionIs your role allowed to do this, and is this record yours? Both must be yes.
5. A reason is always requiredApprove, reject, correction, on hold, send back, take back, reopen: each needs a written comment. The comment stays on the record.
6. Approving is separate from editingBeing allowed to work on a record does not mean being allowed to approve it.
7. Everything is recordedEvery change is stored with who, when and what. The history cannot be edited or deleted by anyone, and every version of every quote is kept.

3. Signing in: every login flow

Nobody can register themselves on the platform. ERGO decides who may use it, which company they belong to and what they may do. This section follows a person from the first invitation to everyday use, and shows every special case.

3.1 How companies and users come onto the platform

ERGO registers the company (draft) → Sent for approval → ERGO Management approves → ERGO Admin creates the user → Temporary password given to the person → First sign-in → Person sets their own password → Works in their role
StepWhat happens
Register the companyCustomers are registered by the ERGO Admin, ERGO RFQ or an ERGO KAM (for their own customers); suppliers by the ERGO Admin or ERGO RFQ (Customers & suppliers screen). Nobody registers themselves. The RFQ number prefix of each customer is set here (for example IC-2026-001), and an ERGO KAM is assigned to each customer account. The company is saved as a draft and can be completed over several visits.
Audit report (suppliers) or assessment (customers)Supplier: ERGO Quality adds an audit report: date, type (on-site, virtual or desk review), auditor, findings, a recommendation (approve or reject) and the report file. At least one is needed before a supplier can be sent for approval. Quality can already see suppliers that are still drafts or waiting, so it can audit them in time. Customer: an audit is not required; the team that registers the customer may add a simple assessment (desk review notes, a risk rating low, medium or high, and an optional file). Entries are kept as history and are never changed; a correction is a new entry. They are seen only inside ERGO, never by the customer or the supplier.
ApprovalEvery new company, customer or supplier, starts as a draft and gets an automatic number (CUS-00001, SUP-00001). When the minimum information is filled in (country, address, registration or tax number, a main contact with email, and for a supplier its audit report), the person who registered it presses Send for approval. ERGO Management alone then approves it, rejects it (with a reason) or asks for more information (with a question); the registering person is notified, completes the company and sends it again. Whoever registered the company or sent it for approval cannot approve it themselves, and it cannot be changed while Management is deciding. Management's comments are seen by Management and, while a company is sent back or rejected, by the people who must act on them. Only an approved company can have users, and only an approved supplier can be sent parts. Companies already in the system stay active; the screen shows what is still missing on them.
Create the userOnly the ERGO Admin creates users, each with a company and one or more roles. The system generates a temporary password, shown to the Admin once and never stored in readable form. The Admin passes it to the person.
First sign-inWith the temporary password the person is taken to the Change password screen before anything else. They choose their own password (at least 10 characters). Until they do, no other screen opens.
Company profileCustomers and suppliers maintain their own company profile (the Company profile screen). ERGO maintains everything else about them.

A1. The customer creates the RFQ

Customer RFQ Customer KAM   Screen: RFQs → New RFQ

A request for quotation is the customer's enquiry: "please quote for these parts". One RFQ holds one or many parts.

* required. The customer can save a draft and come back later (status Draft), or copy an earlier RFQ. A duplicate of the same part number and revision in one RFQ is not accepted. If the customer leaves the form with unsaved changes, the system warns first.

No process flow on the RFQ. The customer describes the part; it does not plan the manufacturing route. A supplier may add its own process flow to its quote (step A4), but it is optional.

Customer's view of the status: Draft → Submitted → Under Review → Quotation Submitted, then On hold, Correction required, Approved, Rejected, Closed or Cancelled. After submitting, the customer can edit the RFQ again only if ERGO sends it back for clarification.

A2. ERGO reviews the RFQ as the middle man

ERGO RFQ ERGO Management ERGO KAM (view)   Screen: RFQs → open the RFQ

ERGO opens the submitted RFQ and presses Start review (status Under Review). The RFQ page has tabs: Overview (all parts with their files), Parts & suppliers, Supplier quotations and, once a quotation has been sent, Customer quotation.

  • Replace a file the customer uploaded
  • Plan the process flow for the supplier (the supplier does that in its own quote)
If a change is needed, ERGO uses Clarification Required and the customer makes the change.
A3. ERGO sends parts to one or several suppliers

ERGO RFQ   Screen: RFQ → Parts & suppliers

  1. Tick the parts to send (or "Select all parts that can be sent"). Each part shows which suppliers already hold it and the state of each.
  2. Press Send selected parts to suppliers. In the dialog, choose one or several suppliers (only approved suppliers are listed; a supplier that already holds the part is not offered again).
  3. Set the "Quotes due by" date (required).
  4. Choose the files to share. Every file the customer attached to these parts is ticked to start with; ERGO unticks anything that should not go to suppliers, for example a drawing carrying the customer's logo. Sharing works per part: a file shared for a part stays visible to all suppliers of that part.
  5. Press Send. Each supplier gets its own request.

What a supplier receives: only the part(s) sent to it: part number, revision, description, technical data and the shared files, under a neutral reference such as RFQ-2026-00123. Never the customer's name or RFQ number, the target prices, the other suppliers, or that other suppliers exist. Each supplier's own request is numbered from 1.

A part can be sent to several suppliers at once, and parts can be split between suppliers (for example 3 machined parts to supplier A, 2 plastic parts to B, 2 castings to C and the same casting also to D). A part can be sent to one supplier only once; ERGO can take a part back from a supplier (A5) and send it again later.

RFQ status: Sent to Suppliers, then Quotes Partial and Quotes Complete as the parts receive quotes. Progress is counted per part, not per supplier.

If ERGO asks for a revision, the supplier sees ERGO's comments, edits the quote and resubmits (status Resubmitted). If ERGO takes the part back, the supplier no longer sees it.

A5. ERGO compares the supplier quotes

ERGO RFQ   Screen: RFQ → Supplier quotations

For each part, one table shows every supplier's quote side by side: supplier, status, due date, version, piece price, price converted to the RFQ currency (using the exchange rates ERGO maintains), tooling cost, MOQ, production lead time and valid-until date. The lowest price is highlighted. The same table lists the quotes still being prepared, so ERGO sees who has answered and who has not.

Every action on a quote is in the three-dots menu (⋮) at the end of its row. The menu lists only what is allowed at that moment:

Menu itemWhat it does
OpenOpens the supplier's full quote with all versions, the remarks and the process flow.
Select for customerChooses this supplier's quote for the part (status Accepted). Only one quote per part can be selected. If another quote was already selected, the system asks "Select X instead?" and, on confirming, moves the selection; the earlier one returns to Submitted.
Send to customerAvailable after a quote is selected. Opens the quotation dialog (A6). A button above the table, "Send all N selected quotes to the customer", sends several parts in one quotation.
Revision requiredSends that supplier's quote back with a mandatory comment. The supplier edits and resubmits; the earlier version stays on record.
Take backTakes the part away from that supplier, with a mandatory comment. The supplier no longer sees it; its quote stays on record. ERGO can then send the part to another supplier.
A6. ERGO selects one quote and sends ERGO's own quotation to the customer

ERGO RFQ   Screen: Supplier quotations → ⋮ → Send to customer

In the quotation dialog ERGO enters its own customer-facing terms on top of the selected supplier quote: currency, production piece price, PPAP piece price, tooling cost, MOQ, lead times, incoterm, payment terms, valid-until date and remarks for the customer. The system builds ERGO's quotation in the customer's format and sends it. The RFQ becomes Sent to Customer, and a new Customer quotation tab appears on the RFQ (it is not shown before a quotation exists).

What reaches the customer: only the quotation for the selected quote, at ERGO's price. The supplier's own document is never forwarded, the other suppliers' quotes are never sent, and ERGO's margin is never shown. ERGO keeps both documents for traceability. The system refuses to put a quote that has not been selected into a quotation.

Worked example. Part PN-100 goes to Precision Castings and Shakti Forgings. Precision quotes 90, Shakti quotes 80 (in the RFQ currency). ERGO compares them, selects Shakti's, and sends the customer a quotation at 100. The customer sees one line at 100. Precision still sees its own quote as "Submitted" and is not told anything.

A7. The customer reviews the quotation and decides

Customer RFQ Customer KAM (correction only)   Screen: RFQ → Customer quotation

For each part the customer chooses one of four answers, and every decision needs a comment:

DecisionWhat happens
ApproveThe part is approved and can go to a Sample PO (Stage B). Status: Approved.
Correction requiredThe quotation goes back to ERGO RFQ. ERGO can adjust the price and resend, or even select a different supplier's quote. Status: Correction required.
RejectThe part is declined. Status: Rejected.
On holdReceived, but the decision is paused. The customer answers once more later.

Only the customer's approving role may approve, reject or hold; lower roles can only request a correction.

A8. After the customer decides

System

  • When the customer approves or rejects a part, the quotes of the other suppliers for that part that were still unfinished are closed. A supplier whose quote is closed sees it as an unsent draft that cannot be changed; it is never told that a competitor exists.
  • Suppliers whose quote was submitted but not selected stay "Submitted" and are not told the outcome.
  • Once a quote has gone to the customer, ERGO can no longer select another quote, send another quote back or add a supplier for that part, until the customer answers. After a "Correction required" answer, ERGO can work on it again.
  • The supplier whose quote was selected sees only Accepted. It is not told what the customer decided.

5.2 Step by step

B1. The customer raises a Sample PO

Customer NPD   Screen: NPD → Sample POs → New

For a part approved in Stage A, the Customer NPD team raises a Sample Purchase Order: PO number, sample quantity and required date. It is submitted to the ERGO KAM of the account. There can be only one open Sample PO per part.

Status: Submitted → Returned (by the KAM, with a comment; the customer corrects and resubmits) or Accepted (project created).

B2. The KAM creates the sample project

ERGO KAM ERGO Management   Screen: NPD → Sample POs → open the PO

The KAM checks the Sample PO and either returns it with a comment, or presses Create project, assigning one ERGO NPD person and one ERGO Quality person. For customers without an assigned KAM, ERGO Management does this. The project now exists and shows on the ERGO NPD person's dashboard. The supplier cannot see it yet.

B3. ERGO builds the plan and releases it to the supplier

ERGO NPD ERGO Quality   Screen: NPD → project → Plan

ERGO NPD discusses the project with ERGO Quality and builds the NPD plan: the steps needed, such as raw-material availability, tooling readiness, sample manufacturing date and packaging design. ERGO NPD then presses Assign plan to supplier, releasing it to the supplier's NPD, RFQ and Quality teams. Until it is released, the supplier cannot see the project at all.

B4. The supplier submits the plan, and ERGO agrees

Supplier NPD ERGO NPD   Screen: NPD Tasks → project

The supplier fills in its steps (planned dates, owners, remarks) and presses Submit plan. ERGO NPD either agrees it, or sends it back with a mandatory comment (the supplier corrects and resubmits). No work can be recorded before the plan is agreed.

Plan status: Awaiting plan → Assigned to supplier → Submitted → Changes requested (back to the supplier) or Agreed.

B5. Execution through nine stages

ERGO NPD Supplier NPD Customer NPD (follows)

Planning → Commercial and sourcing readiness → Tooling and material release → Trial preparation → Trial execution → Final sample readiness → Customer feedback → Approved for production handover → Closed

Inside each stage the teams work through activities with dates and owners, checklists and issues, and mark them done. Progress is calculated automatically, and a stage can be closed only when its steps are done. Safeguards: Final sample readiness cannot be closed without a real inspection and both quality approvals, and Customer feedback cannot be closed without the customer's approval.

B6. The quality gate: Supplier Quality and ERGO Quality

Supplier Quality (two people) ERGO Quality

  1. When the development steps are complete, Supplier Quality inspects and approves internally, then submits the project for validation.
  2. Both ERGO Quality and a second Supplier Quality engineer must approve. The person who submitted cannot approve their own submission.
  3. If either rejects, a reason is required and the project goes back to the relevant development stage for rework, with the failure comments. All rounds are kept in the history.

Quality gate status: Not started → In validation → Validated → In customer review → Customer approved.

B7. The customer's final approval

Customer NPD Customer Quality

After both quality approvals, the sample goes to the customer. Both Customer NPD and Customer Quality must approve. If either rejects (reason required), the project returns to the quality stage with comments for correction and resubmission. When both approve, the project is Approved for Production Handover and the customer can release a Production PO.

C1. The Production PO

Customer OFP ERGO KAM   Screen: Orders → Production POs

For an approved sample, the Customer OFP team releases a production purchase order (PO number, quantity, dates). The ERGO KAM is notified, checks it and either returns it with a comment or creates the production project, assigning an ERGO OFP person and an ERGO Quality person. Status: Submitted → Returned or Accepted.

C2. The production project

ERGO OFP Customer OFP Supplier OFP

ERGO OFP builds a project like the sample project but with only the relevant steps: the stages Tooling and material release and Trial preparation are skipped, and there is no tool development or sample approval. ERGO OFP adds the Customer OFP and Supplier OFP teams and releases the plan to the supplier. The same cycle follows: supplier plan, ERGO agreement, execution, dual quality gate and customer approval. The customer's OFP team takes part in the project; final approval stays with Customer NPD and Customer Quality.

C3. The order and its tracking

ERGO OFP Supplier OFP   Screen: Orders

ERGO OFP creates the order, which can only be made from a production project and takes its details from the Production PO. The order moves through these statuses; the supplier updates material, production and packaging, and ERGO OFP follows and controls:

Order created → Material ordered → Material received → Production started → In inspection → Packaging in progress → Dispatch ready → Dispatched → In transit → Delivered → Closed

An order can be put On hold with a reason and released again. Each order also tracks material status, production progress, packaging and dispatch details, and shipments.

C4. Inspection gate, dispatch, delivery and closure

Supplier OFP ERGO OFP ERGO Quality Customer OFP

  • Goods cannot be dispatched until the inspection has passed. A failed inspection blocks dispatch until it is resolved by a non-conformance report (7.2) or an approved deviation (7.3).
  • Shipments are recorded (dispatched, in transit, delivered) and the proof of delivery is uploaded. The customer sees its own shipments.
  • ERGO OFP confirms delivery and closes the order.

7. Quality processes

Quality records belong to orders and projects. Their results control the gates above: a failed inspection blocks dispatch, and a project cannot pass the quality gate without real inspections. Each side sees only its own records.

7.1 Inspection

Open → In inspection → Passed / Rejected / Released with deviation → Closed

Raised and run by ERGO Quality or Supplier Quality (and followed by the customer's Quality team). A closed inspection can be reopened with a reason.

7.2 Non-conformance report (NCR) and corrective action (CAPA)

Open → Containment → Root cause → Corrective and preventive action → Waiting for closure approval → Closed

A failed part gets an NCR. The supplier and ERGO Quality work through containment, root cause and corrective action, with evidence files on each action. Closure needs approval. An NCR can be sent back or reopened with a reason. Overdue actions are reminded daily.

7.3 Deviation

Requested → Under review → Customer approval (when needed) → Approved / Rejected → Expired / Closed

A deviation allows slightly-off parts to be shipped, with approval. The customer's Quality team approves where the customer must. An approved deviation expires automatically on its end date.

8. Supporting functions

8.1 Documents

One library for all files (screen: Documents). Each file has a category and versions, and can be replaced by a new version, locked, approved or rejected. Files attached to an RFQ, project or order follow the access rules of that record. Files that ERGO adds stay internal until ERGO shares them, and a customer's files on an RFQ cannot be replaced by ERGO.
