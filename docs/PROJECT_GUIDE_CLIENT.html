ERGO Connect

Platform guide: how it works, who does what, and where it stands  ·  November 2026

1. What is ERGO Connect?

ERGO Connect is one web platform where ERGO, its customers (who need parts) and its suppliers (who make parts) work together, from the first request for a price up to the delivered and closed order. Everyone logs in to the same system, but each person sees only what belongs to their company and their job.

RFQ→Quotation→Sample PO→Plan→ Execution→Quality check→Customer approval→ Production PO→Delivery→Closed
The golden rule. ERGO always stays in the middle. The customer never sees who the supplier is. The supplier never sees who the customer is, what the customer hopes to pay, what other suppliers quoted, or ERGO's margin.

4. Who sees what

Customer sees

  • Its own RFQs and its own target price
  • ERGO's quotation only
  • Its own Sample POs and Production POs
  • Project status and its own decisions
  • Its own orders and shipments
Never: supplier names or supplier quotes

ERGO sees

  • Both the customer side and the supplier side
  • Supplier quotes and ERGO's margin
  • Internal notes
  • Full plan and quality history
  • Each person by role; a KAM only the assigned accounts

Supplier sees

  • Only the parts sent to it, under a neutral reference
  • Its own quotes and ERGO's comments
  • Its own plan, steps and quality feedback
  • Its own orders
Never: customer name, target price, other suppliers' quotes, ERGO's margin

This separation is built into the system itself. It does not depend on hiding buttons on the screen.

6. What the platform includes

RFQ. One screen to create a request with many parts, four file slots per part (2D drawing, 3D model, specification, additional), save as draft or submit.
Quotation. Send parts to suppliers, collect and review their quotes, generate ERGO's quotation, record the customer's decision per part. Every version is kept.
NPD (sample project). Sample PO, project creation by the KAM, plan agreement with the supplier, step tracking, double quality check, customer final approval.

Notifications. A bell in the app and emails for every important event, plus daily reminders for overdue items.
Dashboards. Separate views for Management, each ERGO team, customers and suppliers, showing what needs attention and the status of each part.
Administration. Users, companies, suppliers, master data (materials, processes, coatings, payment terms, currencies, exchange rates), theme and branding.

7. Each module in detail

7.1 RFQ

The customer's request. One RFQ has a header and a table of parts.

  • Header: title, expected quotation date, currency, description, special instructions.
  • For each part: part number, description, yearly quantity, target price, remarks and four file slots (2D drawing, 3D model, specification, additional). Files can be up to 50 MB each.
  • Screens: RFQ list with search and filters, the create/edit screen, and the RFQ page with an overview of all parts and the status of each one.
  • The customer can: save a draft, submit, copy an earlier RFQ, answer a clarification. After submitting, the RFQ can be edited by the customer only when ERGO sends it back.
  • ERGO can: start the review, complete the technical details, ask for clarification, keep internal notes (never shown outside ERGO), close or cancel.
Status the customer seesMeaning
DraftSaved by the customer, not yet sent to ERGO.
SubmittedSent to ERGO, waiting for the review to start.
Under ReviewERGO is working on it: reviewing, asking suppliers, collecting quotes.
Quotation SubmittedERGO's quotation is with the customer for a decision.
On hold / Correction requiredThe customer has paused its decision, or has asked ERGO to correct the quotation.
Approved / RejectedThe customer's final decision.
Closed / CancelledThe RFQ is finished or was stopped.

ERGO sees more detail inside "Under Review": Clarification required, Sent to suppliers, Quotes partly received, Quotes complete. Once parts are with suppliers, the RFQ status follows the status of its parts automatically.

7.2 Quotation

Two different documents are handled here: the supplier quote (supplier to ERGO) and ERGO's quotation (ERGO to customer).

  • Supplier: has a work list of parts to quote, can save a draft, submit, and resubmit after a revision request. A zero price is not accepted.
  • ERGO: sends parts out, asks for revision, accepts, or takes a part back from a supplier. A part is with one supplier at a time.
  • Generate quotation: ERGO enters its own price and terms on top of the accepted supplier quote and sends it to the customer. There is one open quotation per RFQ at a time.
  • Customer: answers part by part with a comment. "On hold" keeps the part open and the customer gives one more answer later.
  • History: every version of every quote is kept and cannot be changed afterwards.

7.3 NPD: the sample project

  • Sample PO: raised by the customer for an approved part. Status: Submitted, Returned (by the KAM, with a comment) or Accepted (project created). One open Sample PO per part.
  • Plan: ERGO builds the steps and releases them to the supplier; the supplier adds dates and owners; ERGO agrees or sends back. Status: Awaiting plan, Assigned to supplier, Submitted, Changes requested, Agreed. No work can be recorded before the plan is agreed.
  • Stages: Planning → Commercial and sourcing readiness → Tooling and material release → Trial preparation → Trial execution → Final sample readiness → Customer feedback → Approved for production → Closed.
  • Inside each stage: activities with dates and owners, checklists, issues and progress.
  • Quality check: Not started → In validation → Validated (Supplier Quality and ERGO Quality) → In customer review → Customer approved (Customer NPD and Customer Quality). Every rejection needs a reason and starts a new round; all rounds are kept.
  • Safeguards: Final sample readiness cannot be closed without a real inspection and both quality approvals. Customer feedback cannot be closed without the customer's approval.

7.4 OFP: production and orders

  • Production PO: released by the customer after the sample is approved. Same statuses as the Sample PO: Submitted, Returned, Accepted.
  • Production project: works like the sample project, without the tooling and trial-preparation stages.
  • Order: created only from a production project, with details taken from the Production PO.
  • Order tracking: material status, production progress, packaging and dispatch details, shipments with proof of delivery.

7.5 Quality

  • Inspection: Open → In inspection → Passed, Rejected or Released with deviation → Closed. A closed inspection can be reopened with a reason.
  • Non-conformance report (NCR): Open → Containment → Root cause → Corrective and preventive action → Waiting for closure approval → Closed. It can be sent back or reopened with a reason. Evidence files can be attached to each action.
  • Deviation: Requested → Under review → Customer approval (when needed) → Approved or Rejected → Closed. An approved deviation expires automatically on its end date.
  • Link to the flow: quality results control the gates in the sample project and block dispatch of an order until the inspection has passed.

7.6 Documents, communication and notifications

  • Documents: one library for all files. Each file has a category and versions; it can be replaced by a new version, locked, approved or rejected. Files attached to an RFQ, project or order follow the access rules of that record. Files added by ERGO stay internal until ERGO shares them.
  • Comments: every record has its own conversation. Users can mention a colleague, attach files, mark a point as resolved or escalate it. The Communication Center shows all conversations in one inbox.
  • Notifications: the bell and an email inform the right people at each event: RFQ submitted, quote received, PO raised or returned, plan submitted, approval needed, order dispatched, quality result, mention in a comment.
  • Reminders: overdue items are reminded once a day to the responsible person.

7.8 Administration

  • Users: created by the ERGO Admin only, each with a company and one or more roles.
  • Companies and suppliers: customer and supplier companies are registered and approved by ERGO. The RFQ number prefix is set per customer. A KAM is assigned to each customer account.
  • Master data: materials, processes, coatings, payment terms, currencies and exchange rates.
  • Permissions: the access table per role, and the switch per module.
  • Theme and branding: logo and colours, prepared as a draft and then published.

8. Control and safety

  • Two checks on every action: is your role allowed to do this, and is this record yours? Both must be yes.
  • Approval is separate from editing. Being able to work on a record does not mean being able to approve it.
  • A reason is always required to reject, send back, reopen or put on hold.
  • Full history. Every change is recorded with who, when and what. The history cannot be edited or deleted by anyone.
  • Permission Management. The ERGO Admin can change what each role may do from a screen, without any software change. Every change needs a reason and is recorded.
  • Module switches. The Admin can set a whole module to read-only or hide it, for example during a rollout.
  • "Login as". The ERGO Admin can open the system as another user for up to 30 minutes to help or check, with a written reason. The Admin cannot approve or change passwords this way, and every such session is recorded.
  • Secure login. Strong passwords, a one-time code by email as a second step, automatic lock after five wrong passwords, password reset by email, encrypted connection.

12. Common questions

Can a supplier find out which customer a request came from?

No. The supplier sees a neutral reference number, never the customer's name or RFQ number. This also applies to emails, notifications and search.

Can a customer see the supplier's name or price?

No. The customer receives only ERGO's own quotation. The supplier's quote is never forwarded.

Does the supplier learn whether the customer approved?

No. After ERGO accepts a quote, the supplier sees it as "Accepted" and nothing more.

Can one RFQ go to several suppliers?

Yes. The parts of one RFQ can be split between suppliers. Each part is with one supplier at a time.

Can the customer change an RFQ after submitting it?

Only when ERGO sends it back for clarification. Otherwise ERGO completes the details during its review.

What does "On hold" mean for a quotation?

The customer is not ready to decide. The part stays open and the customer gives one more answer later.

Can goods be dispatched without a quality inspection?

No. Dispatch is blocked until the inspection has passed.

Can one person have more than one role?

Yes. The person chooses a role at login and can switch, but works in one role at a time.

Who can change what a role is allowed to do?

Only the ERGO Admin, from the Permission Management screen. Each change needs a reason and is kept in the history.

Can the ERGO Admin approve a quotation or a sample?

No. The Admin manages the system and can view the work, but approvals stay with the business roles, also when using "Login as".

Can the history be changed or deleted?

No. Nobody can edit or delete history entries, including the Admin.
