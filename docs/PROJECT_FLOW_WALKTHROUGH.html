ERGO Connect

Complete process flow, from the customer's RFQ to the production order, with the demo logins to show each step
Prepared for the client demonstration, 21 November 2026

1. The big picture

ERGO sits between customers (who need parts) and suppliers (who make them). The customer never deals with the supplier directly, and the supplier never learns who the customer is. Everything passes through ERGO, which checks, prices and controls every hand-over. The platform records who did what, when and why, so every step can be traced afterwards.

Customer RFQ → ERGO review → Supplier quotation → ERGO checks and prices → Customer quotation → Customer decision
↓
Sample PO → KAM creates project → NPD plan → Supplier submits, ERGO agrees → Execution → Quality gate → Customer final approval → Production PO → OFP project

The three kinds of organisation and their teams

OrganisationTeams (roles)
Customer (buys parts)RFQ team, NPD team, OFP team, Quality team, KAM, Management
ERGO (the company in the middle)RFQ, NPD, OFP, Quality, KAM (Key Account Manager), Management, Admin
Supplier (makes parts)RFQ, NPD, OFP, Quality, KAM

NPD = New Product Development (making the sample). OFP = Order Fulfilment and Production (making the real order). PO = purchase order. KAM = the ERGO person who looks after a customer account.

2. Stage A: from RFQ to customer decision (flow steps 1 to 5)

Step 1. The customer creates the RFQ

Customer RFQ team

A request for quotation (RFQ) is the customer's enquiry: "please quote for these parts". One RFQ can hold several parts (items). For each part the customer gives the part number, description, yearly volume (EAU), material, drawings and the price it hopes to pay. The RFQ starts as a Draft, and the customer submits it when ready (status: Submitted). A title, an expected quote-receipt date and a yearly volume on every part are required.

Demo login: buyer@acme.dev (Acme Auto) or buyer@bharat.dev (Bharat Motors)

Step 2. ERGO reviews the RFQ and sends it to suppliers

ERGO RFQ team

ERGO opens the RFQ (Under Review), checks it against the drawings, and can edit details or ask the customer for clarification (status Clarification Required; the customer answers and resubmits). ERGO then picks suppliers from the supplier list. It can send the whole RFQ to one supplier, or split it into sub-RFQs: for example, 7 parts become 3 machined parts to supplier A, 2 plastic parts to supplier B and 2 castings to supplier C. One part is with one supplier at a time. The supplier sees only its own parts and a neutral reference number, never the customer's name.

Status: Sent to Suppliers. Demo login: rfq@ergo.dev

Step 3. The supplier prepares and submits a quotation

Supplier RFQ team

The supplier reviews the drawings and costs the part: piece price, tooling cost, lead times, minimum order quantity, process steps, remarks. It can save a draft and then submits the quotation to ERGO. There is one quotation per part number, and the dashboard shows the status of every part separately.

Demo logins: sales@precision.dev (Precision Castings), sales@shakti.dev (Shakti Forgings), sales@deccan.dev (Deccan Machining)

Step 4. ERGO reviews the supplier quote and generates the customer quotation

ERGO RFQ team

ERGO reads the supplier's quote and remarks. Two outcomes:

  • Revision required: ERGO sends it back to the supplier with comments; the supplier fixes it and resubmits.
  • Accepted: ERGO uses Generate Quotation for Customer. The system builds ERGO's own customer-facing quotation (with ERGO's price and margin) in the customer's format and sends it.
Key rule: the supplier's quotation is never forwarded as it is, and the customer never sees any supplier quotation. ERGO keeps both documents for traceability. The customer also never sees competitor prices or ERGO's margin.

Status: Quotes Partial / Quotes Complete, then Sent to Customer. Demo login: rfq@ergo.dev

Step 5. The customer reviews the quotation and decides

Customer RFQ team

For each part the customer chooses one of four answers, and every decision needs a comment:

DecisionWhat happens
ApproveThe part is approved and can go to a Sample PO (Stage B).
Correction requiredThe quotation goes back to ERGO RFQ for changes (status Customer Revision Requested).
RejectThe part is declined (status Customer Declined).
On holdReceived, but the decision to move on is paused. The customer answers once more later.

Demo login: buyer@acme.dev. Only the customer's approving role may approve, reject or hold; lower roles can only request a correction.

3. Stage B: Sample development (flow steps 6 to 10)

Step 6. The customer raises a Sample PO

Customer NPD team

Once a part is approved, the Customer NPD team raises a Sample Purchase Order for it (PO number, sample quantity, required date) and submits it to the ERGO KAM.

Demo login: npd@acme.dev. In the demo data, the Sample PO for SAH-SPRING-15 (npd@sahyadri.dev) is raised and waiting.

Step 7. The KAM creates the sample project and assigns ERGO's team

ERGO KAM ERGO NPD ERGO Quality

The KAM reviews the Sample PO and either returns it with a comment or creates the sample development project, assigning one ERGO NPD and one ERGO Quality person. ERGO NPD then discusses the project with ERGO Quality and builds the NPD plan: the steps needed, such as raw-material availability, tooling readiness, sample manufacturing date and packaging design. ERGO NPD then releases the plan to the supplier's NPD, RFQ and Quality teams. Until it is released, the supplier cannot see the project at all.

Demo logins: KAM kam@ergo.dev (Acme only; for Bharat and Sahyadri use management@ergo.dev), ERGO NPD npd@ergo.dev

Step 8. Plan submission, ERGO agreement and execution

Supplier NPD ERGO NPD

The supplier fills in its steps (planned dates, owners, remarks) and submits the plan. ERGO NPD either agrees it or sends it back with a mandatory comment. Once agreed, the project goes into execution: the teams work the steps through nine stages (Planning, Commercial and Sourcing Readiness, Tooling and Material Release, Trial Preparation, Trial Execution, Final Sample Readiness, Customer Feedback, Approved for Production Handover). Progress is calculated automatically, and a stage can be closed only when its steps are done.

Demo logins: tooling@precision.dev or tooling@shakti.dev (supplier NPD), npd@ergo.dev. BHM-HUB-12 is waiting for ERGO to agree the supplier's plan.

Step 9. The quality gate: Supplier Quality and ERGO Quality

Supplier Quality ERGO Quality

When the development steps are complete, Supplier Quality inspects and approves internally, then submits the project. Both ERGO Quality and a (second) Supplier Quality engineer must approve. If ERGO Quality rejects, the project goes back to the relevant development stage for rework, with the failure comments. Every quality decision is recorded in the project history.

Demo logins: qa@precision.dev and qa2@precision.dev (two Precision quality engineers), quality@ergo.dev

Step 10. The customer's final approval

Customer NPD Customer Quality

After both quality approvals, the sample goes to the customer. Both Customer NPD and Customer Quality must approve. If either rejects, the project returns to the quality stage with comments for correction and resubmission. When both approve, the project is Approved for Production Handover.

Demo logins: npd@acme.dev and quality@acme.dev

4. Stage C: Production order and OFP (flow step 11)

Step 11. Production PO, the OFP project and production

Customer OFP ERGO KAM ERGO OFP Supplier OFP

  • The Customer OFP team releases a production purchase order for an approved sample. The ERGO KAM is notified.
  • The KAM accepts it by creating the OFP project (or returns it with a comment), and assigns an ERGO OFP person and an ERGO Quality person.
  • ERGO OFP builds a project like the NPD one but with only the relevant steps (no tool development and no sample approval), adds the Customer OFP and Supplier OFP teams, and releases the plan to the supplier.
  • The same cycle follows: supplier plan, ERGO agreement, execution, dual quality gate and customer approval.
  • After that, ERGO OFP creates the order, tracks production and packaging, and dispatches shipments to the customer until delivery and closure.

Demo logins: logistics@acme.dev (Customer OFP), kam@ergo.dev, ofp@ergo.dev, planner@precision.dev (Supplier OFP). In the demo data, ACM-KNUCKLE-01 has reached an OFP project in execution.

5. Rules that apply everywhere

RuleWhat it means
Nobody sees another side's dataCustomers never see suppliers, supplier quotes, competitor quotes, target prices or ERGO's margin. Suppliers never see the customer. Only ERGO sees both sides. This is enforced on the server, not just hidden in the screens.
Comments on decisionsApprove, reject, correction, on hold, send-back and reopen all require a written reason.
Rework with a trailRejected quotations, failed quality checks and rejected approvals return to the right stage with comments, and the earlier history stays visible.
Approve is separate from editThe ability to change a record and the ability to approve it are separate permissions.
Audit historyEvery change to RFQs, quotations, steps, quality results and decisions is recorded and cannot be altered.
Role-based screensEach person sees only the menu and buttons their role allows.
